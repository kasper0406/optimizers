# T1 — EMA-as-anytime-anneal: refuted on airbench-Muon

Date: 2026-08-02. Companion to `docs/litreview/j-theory-theorem-sweep.md` §5
(theme T1) and `reports/wpj-t1-ema-table.md` / `reports/wpj-t1-ema.json`
(generated by `scripts/analyze_ema.py`, deterministic from results JSONs).

## Claim under test

The averaging↔LR-decay equivalence line (Sandler et al. arXiv:2301.02312;
Defazio et al. arXiv:2405.15682; Hägele et al. arXiv:2405.18392; "Anytime
Pretraining" arXiv:2602.03702) predicts that a weight-EMA maintained during a
constant-LR run substitutes for the anneal — and our F1 measurement (majority
lag-1-anticorrelated per-direction population) made airbench-Muon look like
the ideal substrate, since averaging cancels anticorrelated oscillation
especially fast. Pre-stated predictions (configs/wpj_t1_ema_constant.yaml):
(i) constant-LR EMA final ≈ annealed final; (ii) EMA crosses the annealed
final before the full budget ("harvest epoch" < 8); (iii) constant-LR raw
final < annealed final.

## Design

Experiment `airbench_ema` (commit c5b7cc0 + 644ae39): stock airbench94 recipe
(vendored Muon, lr 0.24, momentum 0.6, compile on), weight-EMA shadows in
fp32 at γ ∈ {0.9, 0.96, 0.98, 0.99} over all params + BN float buffers,
per-epoch EMA eval (tta 0) + final EMA TTA (tta 2), bit-exact swap/restore
(training trajectories untouched; linear arm = the stock baseline
trajectory). Arms: `linear` (stock linear-decay-to-zero) vs `constant` (all
groups held at initial LR; whiten-bias keeps its stock 3-epoch decay). n=20
dev seeds (1000–1019), seed-paired, single GPU type (NVIDIA L40, spot),
8 epochs / 200 steps. Results: `results/airbench_ema_seed10*.json` (+ one
seed-3001 smoke), $0.05/run stamped ($2.00 episode total).

## Results (paired, 95% CI)

- Linear arm final: val 0.9318, TTA 0.9400 (in-band for the stock recipe).
- **(iii) confirmed, violently**: constant-arm raw final −0.248 ± 0.033 below
  the annealed final (0.684 vs 0.932). The anneal is load-bearing.
- **(i) refuted**: best constant-arm EMA (γ=0.9) final TTA 0.8939 —
  −0.046 ± 0.001 below the annealed TTA. Averaging recovers ~21pp of the
  ~25pp the anneal provides, but the last ~4.6pp requires actually annealing.
- **(ii) refuted**: harvest epoch never occurs — 0/20 seeds at every γ.
- On the linear arm, EMA adds nothing on top of the anneal
  (γ=0.9: −0.0001 ± 0.0003 TTA), and mid-run EMA only reaches the run's own
  final accuracy at the final epoch (median self-crossing 8/8) — no early
  harvest there either.
- γ ordering is monotone: longer horizons are strictly worse at this run
  length (γ=0.99 is catastrophic, −0.31); the best horizon is the shortest
  tested, suggesting the useful averaging window here is ≲ 10 steps —
  consistent with plain last-iterate dominance, not with an anneal-equivalent
  long average.

## Interpretation

On this benchmark the averaging↔decay equivalence does not hold even
approximately. Three regime differences from the papers' settings, any of
which may be decisive: (a) the constant arm runs at the *tuned peak* LR of a
200-step recipe — far hotter relative to its horizon than an LLM stable
phase; the stationary oscillation is wide enough that its mean is simply not
a good point; (b) the vendored Muon renormalizes filter weights every step
(projection to a sphere) and the EMA of sphere points is off-sphere — the
per-minibatch quadratic model behind the Sandler map does not describe this
dynamics; (c) 200 steps leaves no scale separation between EMA horizon,
oscillation period, and run length.

This null is *consistent with* the repo's broader finding that the anneal
does mechanistic work rather than merely revealing accumulated progress: F1's
occupancy collapses during the anneal, and nanogpt's per-loss progress is
cooldown-concentrated. If the anneal's contribution were oscillation-noise
masking, averaging would have captured it; it did not.

## Consequences for the plan

- The "constant-LR + EMA + occupancy-triggered *stop*" arm proposed for the
  occupancy-cooldown pre-registration is **dead at airbench scale**; the
  occupancy trigger must drive a real anneal (arm ii of the pre-registration
  stands unchanged).
- The T1 row of the connection map moves to *refuted (airbench regime)*.
  The nanogpt regime (long stable phase, no per-step renorm, WD=0) is closer
  to the papers' domain, so the equivalence could still hold there — but this
  is now a hypothesis needing its own test, not a default.
- The negative result is worth a paragraph in the measurement paper: it
  cleanly separates "anneal as noise-removal" (refuted here) from "anneal as
  mechanism" (supported), using an instrument (paired EMA readouts) the
  equivalence literature itself proposes.

## Caveats

- Arm difference is all-groups (bias/head SGD held constant too, except
  whiten-bias); a Muon-only-constant arm would isolate the optimizer. Given
  the −25pp raw gap the conclusion is unlikely to flip, but the clean
  ablation is cheap if wanted.
- Constant LR was the peak (0.24); a milder constant LR + EMA might land
  closer to the annealed final (the theory's equivalence maps EMA horizons to
  *some* decay schedule, not necessarily the tuned one). Untested.
- L40 vs the A6000 used for earlier airbench tables: no cross-table
  comparisons are made here; both arms share the GPU type (aggregate
  discipline respected).
